Policy stringency and economic outcomes (2020–2022)

What this is

  • Reproducible Python pipeline building a country–year panel (2020–2022).
  • Descriptive associations (not causal effects).
  • Headline outcome: unemployment. Supporting outcome: GDP growth.

Data

  • OWID COVID compact (daily): stringency index, cases/deaths per million, vaccination coverage.
  • World Bank WDI (annual): GDP growth and unemployment.
  • WDI codes: NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG (GDP growth), SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS (unemployment).

Methods

  • Pooled OLS with year fixed effects.
  • Country fixed effects with standard errors clustered by country.
  • Robustness: drop 2020 and re-estimate FE + clustered models (2021–2022 only).

Links

  • Code + outputs: policy-stringency-econ-outcomes (GitHub)

Results snapshot (brief)

  • Unemployment (country fixed effects, SE clustered by country): a 10-point higher annual stringency index is associated with about +0.15 percentage points higher unemployment (p = 0.006; N = 506).
  • Robustness (drop 2020): the association remains positive (~+0.11 pp per 10 points) but is less precise in 2021–2022 only (p = 0.107; N = 337).
  • GDP growth (supporting): the negative association in 2020–2022 is not robust when excluding 2020 (relationship concentrated in the initial shock year).

GDP growth vs stringency (pooled trend)

GDP results are more sensitive to the sample window: the relationship weakens strongly when excluding 2020.

Notes / limitations

  • Associations only: policy stringency responds to pandemic conditions and other country-specific factors.
  • Annual averaging can dilute timing effects (short intense restrictions vs long mild restrictions).
  • Cross-country data quality/reporting differences may affect comparability.
